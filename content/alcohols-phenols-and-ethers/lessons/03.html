From coal tar to the cumene plant

In 1834 the German chemist Friedlieb Runge distilled the black, sticky tar left over from making coal gas and pulled out a crystalline solid that smelled faintly medicinal. He called it carbolic acid. That is phenol, and for most of the next century coal tar was its only source.

Then phenol became too useful to scrape out of tar. In 1907 Leo Baekeland heated it with formaldehyde and made Bakelite, the first fully synthetic plastic. Today phenol goes into polycarbonate (spectacle lenses, water bottles), epoxy glues, nylon, dyes, antiseptics — and the salicylic acid that becomes aspirin (Lesson 8). Millions of tonnes a year are needed, and almost all of it now comes from one clever process, the last of the four you need to know.

Benzene’s ring is electron-rich: it reacts with electrophiles. But $\ce{OH-}$ is a nucleophile, and it has no reason to attack a ring that is already full of electrons. Every route below solves this by putting a good leaving group on the ring first — Cl, $\ce{SO3H}$, $\ce{N2+}$ — or by sneaking the oxygen in through a completely different door, as the cumene process does.

Four roads to phenol

1. From haloarenes. Chlorobenzene is fused with sodium hydroxide at 623 K and 300 atm; acidifying the sodium phenoxide gives phenol. The conditions are brutal because the aryl C–Cl bond has partial double-bond character (the Cl lone pair is shared with the ring) and sits on an sp² carbon — it simply refuses to be pushed out.

2. From benzenesulphonic acid. Benzene is sulphonated with oleum; the sulphonic acid is fused with sodium hydroxide, and acidification of the sodium phenoxide gives phenol.

3. From diazonium salts. Aniline treated with nitrous acid ($\ce{NaNO2 + HCl}$) at 273–278 K gives benzenediazonium chloride. Warm it with water — or treat it with dilute acid — and nitrogen gas bubbles off, leaving phenol:

$$\ce{C6H5N2+Cl- + H2O ->[warm] C6H5OH + N2 ^ + HCl}$$

4. From cumene (isopropylbenzene). Cumene is oxidised by air to cumene hydroperoxide, which dilute acid splits into phenol and acetone. Two valuable products from benzene and propene — which is exactly why this is how the world makes phenol.

Acid protonates the terminal –OH of the –O–OH group, which leaves as water. As it leaves, the phenyl group migrates from carbon onto the electron-poor oxygen, giving an oxocarbenium ion $\ce{C6H5-O-\overset{+}{C}(CH3)2}$. Water adds to that carbon, and the resulting hemiacetal falls apart into $\ce{C6H5OH}$ and $\ce{(CH3)2C=O}$. The key step — a group hopping from carbon to oxygen — is the same idea as the carbocation shifts of Lesson 2.

When hydroxide attacks the carbon bearing Cl, the ring briefly carries a negative charge. A nitro group ortho or para to that carbon can take the charge onto its own oxygens, stabilising the intermediate and lowering the barrier. So the conditions soften steadily: plain chlorobenzene needs 623 K and 300 atm (IV); one para-nitro group, 443 K (III); two, 368 K (II); three, just warm water (I). Answer (C).

 In the preparation of phenol from cumene in the presence of air, the intermediate formed is: Cumene hydroperoxide 2-Phenylpropan-2-ol Isopropyl phenyl ether Acetophenone

Oxygen from air attacks the benzylic C–H of cumene (it is tertiary and benzylic, so the radical formed there is especially stable), giving cumene hydroperoxide, $\ce{C6H5C(CH3)2-O-O-H}$. Acid then cleaves it to phenol and acetone. Answer (A).

The $\ce{-N2+}$ group is one of the best leaving groups in chemistry: it departs as stable nitrogen gas. Water takes its place, and loss of $\ce{H+}$ gives phenol. Answer (A).

You run a factory with an unlimited supply of benzene, propene and air, and a little acid. Write the three steps that turn them into phenol — and name the second product you can sell.

Step 1 — alkylation: benzene + propene with an acid catalyst ($\ce{H3PO4}$ or $\ce{AlCl3}$) → cumene, $\ce{C6H5CH(CH3)2}$. (Friedel–Crafts: propene is protonated to the 2° isopropyl cation, which attacks the ring.)

Step 2 — air oxidation: cumene + $\ce{O2}$ → cumene hydroperoxide.

Step 3 — acid cleavage: dilute acid → phenol + acetone. Acetone is the bonus product.
  • Chlorobenzene + NaOH at 623 K, 300 atm, then acid (Dow). Nitro groups at o/p make it much easier: one → 443 K, two → 368 K, three → warm water.
  • Benzenesulphonic acid (from oleum) fused with NaOH, then acid.
  • Diazonium salt (aniline + $\ce{NaNO2/HCl}$, 273–278 K) warmed with water → phenol + $\ce{N2}$.
  • Cumene + air → cumene hydroperoxide → dilute acid → phenol + acetone.
