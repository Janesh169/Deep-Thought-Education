The case of the four bottles

Four unlabelled bottles, A to D. A label maker’s receipt says only that each contains a different compound of formula $\ce{C4H10O}$.

  • A does nothing at all when a piece of sodium is dropped in.
  • B turns the Lucas reagent cloudy the instant it is added.
  • C gives a yellow precipitate with iodine and sodium hydroxide.
  • D, passed over copper at 573 K, gives an aldehyde; D’s carbon chain is branched.
 Before solving the case: how many structural isomers have the formula $\ce{C4H10O}$? Count the alcohols (four carbons, one –OH) and the ethers (split four carbons between two groups) separately.

Alcohols (4): butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-1-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol. Ethers (3): ethoxyethane, 1-methoxypropane, 2-methoxypropane. Total 7.

Identify A, B, C and D. Commit to an answer before you reveal.

A: no reaction with sodium means no O–H — an ether (ethoxyethane, 1-methoxypropane or 2-methoxypropane; the clues cannot tell which).

B: instant Lucas turbidity means tertiary; the only 3° $\ce{C4H10O}$ is 2-methylpropan-2-ol.

C: iodoform means a $\ce{CH3CH(OH)-}$ unit: butan-2-ol.

D: an aldehyde over hot copper means primary; branched: 2-methylpropan-1-ol.

 Which sequence converts phenol into aspirin? (i) NaOH; (ii) $\ce{CO2}$, 400 K, 4–7 atm; (iii) $\ce{H+}$; (iv) $\ce{(CH3CO)2O}$, $\ce{H+}$ (i) $\ce{CHCl3}$, NaOH; (ii) $\ce{H+}$; (iii) $\ce{(CH3CO)2O}$ (i) Zn dust, heat; (ii) $\ce{CH3COCl}$, $\ce{AlCl3}$ (i) conc. $\ce{HNO3}$; (ii) Sn, HCl; (iii) $\ce{(CH3CO)2O}$

Aspirin is acetylsalicylic acid. Kolbe’s reaction (phenoxide + $\ce{CO2}$, then acid) gives salicylic acid, and acetylating its –OH gives aspirin. (B) makes salicylaldehyde, so its acetylation gives an aldehyde, not an acid. (C) makes acetophenone. (D) makes an acetylated aminophenol, not aspirin. Answer (A).

 Ethanol and phenol can be distinguished by: neutral $\ce{FeCl3}$ solution bromine water sodium metal $\ce{I2}$ and NaOH

(A) phenol gives violet, ethanol nothing. (B) phenol gives a white precipitate, ethanol nothing. (C) both give hydrogen — no distinction. (D) ethanol gives yellow iodoform, phenol does not. Answer: A, B, D.

 How many of these reagents can convert propan-2-ol into propanone? Acidified $\ce{K2Cr2O7}$, PCC, $\ce{CrO3}$, Cu at 573 K, $\ce{LiAlH4}$, $\ce{NaBH4}$, $\ce{B2H6}$

Propan-2-ol → propanone is an oxidation (dehydrogenation) of a secondary alcohol. Acidified dichromate, PCC, $\ce{CrO3}$ and hot copper all do it — 4. $\ce{LiAlH4}$, $\ce{NaBH4}$ and $\ce{B2H6}$ are reducing agents or hydroborating agents; they go the other way, if anywhere.
  1. 1°/2°/3° = carbons on the carbinol carbon. C–O–H 108.9°, C–O–C 111.7°, phenol C–O 136 pm.
  2. $\ce{H2O/H+}$ Markovnikov (may rearrange); $\ce{B2H6}$/$\ce{H2O2}$ anti-Markovnikov orientation.
  3. Grignard: HCHO → 1°, RCHO → 2°, ketone and ester → 3°.
  4. Phenol from chlorobenzene (623 K, 300 atm), sulphonic acid, diazonium salt, and cumene (+ acetone).
  5. Boiling: alcohol ≫ ether ≈ alkane; solubility: alcohol ≈ ether. o-Nitrophenol is steam volatile.
  6. Acidity: RCOOH > picric acid … > phenol > water > 1° > 2° > 3° alcohol. EWG at o/p helps most.
  7. Lucas: 3° at once, 2° 5 min, 1° no. Dehydration: 3° easiest; carbocation; Saytzeff; watch shifts.
  8. PCC stops at aldehyde; Cu 573 K gives aldehyde/ketone/alkene; iodoform for $\ce{CH3CH(OH)-}$.
  9. Phenol ring: bromine water → tribromo; Kolbe → salicylic acid; Reimer–Tiemann ($\ce{:CCl2}$) → salicylaldehyde.
  10. Williamson: bulky alkoxide + small halide. HI cleaves ethers: small group gets I — unless the other is 3°; anisole → phenol + $\ce{CH3I}$.
