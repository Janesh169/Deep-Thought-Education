Not every material plays by the metal rules

Heat a metal: the same number of carriers, but more collisions — resistance rises. Heat a semiconductor: far more carriers shaken loose — resistance falls. A hot metal is a corridor that gets more crowded; hot silicon is a corridor where new doors keep opening.
