One dead bulb, one dark string

Old strings of festival lights had a maddening habit. One tiny bulb burns out and the whole string goes dark — and you end up testing forty bulbs to find the guilty one.

The bulbs were wired in series: one path for the current, through every bulb in turn. A single broken filament opens the path, and every bulb loses its current.

The lights in your home never do that. Switch off the fan and the tube light stays on, because each appliance is connected across the same two mains wires — in parallel. Each has its own path.

Two ways of joining things, two completely different personalities. This lesson gives you the two rules — and then three tricks for the networks that look like neither.

Series shares the same Current; Parallel shares the same Voltage. If it helps: “Some Cats Prefer Violins.” Everything else in this card follows from those two facts.

Find something at least six metres away and look at it for twenty seconds. Close-up work locks your focusing muscles; distance releases them.
