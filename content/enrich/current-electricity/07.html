The hiss of the tyre gauge

Press a tyre gauge onto a valve and you hear a short hiss. To measure the pressure, the gauge lets out a little of the very air it is measuring — so the number you read belongs to a tyre a touch softer than the one you had.

Every instrument does something like this. A thermometer dipped into a cup of tea cools the tea slightly. An ammeter adds a little resistance to the wire it is put into. A voltmeter steals a little current from the circuit it is watching.

This lesson builds both meters out of one delicate instrument, the galvanometer — and shows how to keep that hiss as quiet as possible.

Ammeter: a small resistance beside the galvanometer — the shunt, in parallel. Voltmeter: a very big resistance in line with it — in series. The ideal ammeter has zero resistance; the ideal voltmeter, infinite.

Rub your palms together until they are warm and rest them lightly over closed eyes for three breaths.
