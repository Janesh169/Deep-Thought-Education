A 27-kilometre steering wheel

Deep under the border of France and Switzerland, protons race round the 27 km ring of the Large Hadron Collider at more than 99.99% of the speed of light.

More than a thousand superconducting magnets line that tunnel — and not one of them speeds the protons up. Their only job is to bend the path, a little at a time, so the beam comes back round to where it started. The pushing is done separately, by electric fields in a few special cavities.

Magnets steer; electric fields push. That division of labour is the whole of this lesson, and it is how every particle accelerator works, from a cyclotron you could hold in your hand to the LHC.

$r = \dfrac{mv}{qB}$ grows with $v$, but $T = \dfrac{2\pi m}{qB}$ has no $v$ in it at all. A faster particle runs a bigger circle in exactly the same time — the fact the whole cyclotron is built on.

Find something at least six metres away and look at it for twenty seconds.

When you come back: a charge that enters the field at a slant does not go round in a circle — it corkscrews. That corkscrew is why the aurora lives at the poles.
