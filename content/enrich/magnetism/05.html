The cone in your earphones

Take the grille off a loudspeaker and you find a paper cone glued to a small coil of wire, which sits in the gap of a strong permanent magnet.

Music arrives as a current that reverses direction hundreds or thousands of times a second. Every reversal flips the force on the coil; the coil drags the cone back and forth; the cone pushes the air — and you hear sound.

Every earphone you have worn is a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field, being shoved sideways. This lesson works out how hard: for straight wires, bent wires, and two wires that pull on each other.

Charges: like repel. Currents: like attract. The two rules are exact opposites — which is exactly why examiners like to test them side by side.

When we concentrate on a screen we blink far less than usual. Blink slowly ten times, then look out of a window if you can.
