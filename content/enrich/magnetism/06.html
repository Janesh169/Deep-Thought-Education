Every motor is this lesson

The ceiling fan above you, the mixer in the kitchen, the motor in an electric scooter: open any of them and you find coils of wire sitting in a magnetic field — and turning.

But Lesson 5 proved that a closed loop in a uniform field feels no net force. So how does anything turn?

Because zero force is not the same as zero twist. Push the two sides of a steering wheel in opposite directions: it does not move anywhere, but it certainly turns. That twist — the torque on a current loop — is what this lesson is about.

$\vec\tau = \vec m \times \vec B$ and $U = -\vec m \cdot \vec B$ are the electric dipole’s formulas, $\vec\tau = \vec p \times \vec E$ and $U = -\vec p \cdot \vec E$, with $p \to m$ and $E \to B$. Learn one set and you get the other free — including stable equilibrium when $\vec m$ points along $\vec B$.

Rest your hands in your lap, close your eyes, and take three slow breaths.

Next: you do not need a wire to make a magnet. A spinning charged disc, or an electron going round a nucleus, is a current loop too.
