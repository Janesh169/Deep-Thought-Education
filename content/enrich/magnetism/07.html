The needle and the mirror

Before digital multimeters, every school and college lab measured current with a needle swinging over a printed scale — and many of those scales had a thin strip of mirror set into them.

The mirror was there to defeat parallax. You moved your head until the needle covered its own reflection; only then was your eye exactly above the needle, and only then did you read the number.

Inside the box was the instrument of this lesson, the moving-coil galvanometer: the torque from Lesson 6, balanced against a spring.

Current sensitivity $= \dfrac{NAB}{k}$: more turns, a bigger coil, a stronger magnet — each makes the coil twist further for the same current. The spring constant downstairs fights them. Want a more sensitive meter? Softer spring.

Find the farthest object in view and rest your eyes on it for three slow breaths.
