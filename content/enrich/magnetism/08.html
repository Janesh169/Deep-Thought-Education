The stone that points north

Long before anyone knew what an electric current was, people knew about lodestones — rocks of the iron ore magnetite that pull on iron and, hung from a thread, swing round to point north–south.

By the eleventh century Chinese writers were describing needles rubbed on lodestone and used as compasses, and within two hundred years sailors across Asia and Europe were steering by them.

For seven centuries nobody could say why a stone should behave like that. You can. The answer is the current loops of Lesson 6, hiding inside atoms — and this lesson shows how.

Diamagnetic — dislikes the field: pushed out, $\chi$ small and negative. Paramagnetic — partly likes it: pulled in weakly, $\chi$ small and positive. Ferromagnetic — fiercely likes it: $\chi$ huge.
