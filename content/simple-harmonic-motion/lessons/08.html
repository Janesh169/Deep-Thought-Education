 A particle executes SHM of amplitude $A$ along the x-axis about $x = 0$. When its potential energy equals its kinetic energy, its position is $A/2$ $A/(2\sqrt2)$ $A/\sqrt2$ $A$

$\tfrac12kx^2 = \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12kA^2$ gives $x = A/\sqrt2$. Answer: C.

 A particle in SHM has displacement $x(t) = A\sin(\pi t/90)$, with $t$ in seconds. Find the ratio of its kinetic energy to its potential energy at $t = 210\ \text{s}$ (2 decimal places). Find the phase $\pi t/90$ at $t = 210$, then use $K/U = \cot^2(\text{phase})$.

Phase $= 210\pi/90 = 7\pi/3 = 2\pi + \pi/3$, which is the same as $\pi/3$. $U \propto \sin^2(\pi/3) = \tfrac34$ and $K \propto \cos^2(\pi/3) = \tfrac14$, so $K/U = \tfrac13 \approx 0.33$.

 A body in SHM has period 0.5 s and amplitude 1 cm. Find its average velocity, in cm/s, as it moves from the mean position to half of its amplitude. Time from 0 to $A/2$ is $T/12$.

Displacement $= 0.5\ \text{cm}$ and time $= T/12 = 0.5/12 = 1/24\ \text{s}$, so the average velocity is $0.5 \times 24 = 12\ \text{cm/s}$.

 A coin rests on a horizontal platform that moves up and down in SHM with angular frequency $\omega$. The amplitude is slowly increased. The coin first loses contact with the platform at the highest position, when the amplitude reaches $g/\omega^2$ at the lowest position, when the amplitude reaches $g/\omega^2$ at the mean position, when the amplitude reaches $g/\omega$ at the highest position, when the amplitude reaches $g/\omega$ The coin can be pushed up by the platform but never pulled down by it. Its downward acceleration can’t exceed $g$.

At the top, the platform accelerates downward at $\omega^2A$. The coin can only fall at $g$. Once $\omega^2A > g$, the platform drops away faster than the coin can follow, so contact is lost there first, at $A = g/\omega^2$. At the bottom the platform pushes up harder, so contact is safest there. Answer: A.

 A 1 kg block on a smooth floor is attached to a spring with $k = 100\ \text{N m}^{-1}$. At rest at its mean position, it is given a speed of 2 m/s. What is the amplitude of the resulting SHM, in cm?

$\omega = \sqrt{k/m} = 10\ \text{rad/s}$. At the mean position $v_{\max} = A\omega$, so $A = 2/10 = 0.2\ \text{m} = 20\ \text{cm}$.

 A particle moves as $x = 3\sin 2t + 4\cos 2t$ (metres, seconds). What is its greatest speed, in m/s?

The amplitude is $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5\ \text{m}$ and $\omega = 2\ \text{rad/s}$, so $v_{\max} = A\omega = 10\ \text{m/s}$.
