1. Particle speed ($A\omega$) is not wave speed ($\omega/k$). 2. In a gas at constant temperature, pressure doesn’t change the speed of sound. 3. The frequency never changes on entering a new medium; $v$ and $\lambda$ do. 4. “$n$-th overtone” is the $(n+1)$-th harmonic for strings and open pipes, but the $(2n+1)$-th harmonic for closed pipes. 5. A half-submerged open pipe becomes a closed pipe of half the length, with the same fundamental. 6. In the resonance tube $l_2 > 3l_1$, because of the end correction. 7. Beats give $|f_1 - f_2|$ but not its sign, so use the wax/file/tension clue. 8. In the Doppler formula a moving source and a moving observer are not interchangeable.

 A string 2.0 m long, fixed at both ends, is driven by a 240 Hz vibrator and vibrates in its third harmonic. The speed of the wave and the fundamental frequency are 160 m/s, 80 Hz 320 m/s, 80 Hz 320 m/s, 120 Hz 180 m/s, 120 Hz

Third harmonic: $L = 3\lambda/2$, so $\lambda = 4/3$ m and $v = f\lambda = 240 \times 4/3 = 320$ m/s. The fundamental is $240/3 = 80$ Hz. Answer: B.

 The first overtone of an open pipe 60 cm long is in unison (has the same frequency) with the fundamental of a closed pipe. How long is the closed pipe, in cm? (Ignore end corrections.)

Open pipe, first overtone: $2v/(2 \times 0.6) = v/0.6$. Closed pipe fundamental: $v/4L$. Setting them equal gives $L = 0.6/4 = 0.15$ m $= 15$ cm.

 Two identical sirens, both at rest, each sound 340 Hz. A person runs at 5 m/s along the line joining them, towards one and away from the other. How many beats per second do they hear? ($v = 340$ m/s)

Towards: $340 \times 345/340 = 345$ Hz. Away: $340 \times 335/340 = 335$ Hz. Beats $= 10$ per second.

 A standing wave is $y = 4\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{15}\right)\cos(96\pi t)$, with $x$ and $y$ in cm. What is the distance, in cm, from a node to the next antinode?

$k = \pi/15$, so $\lambda = 2\pi/k = 30$ cm. Node to antinode is $\lambda/4 = 7.5$ cm.
